How You See & Hear
the World

Your eye is a living camera and your ear is a living microphone. Once you see them as machines with a job to do, every part and every defect suddenly makes sense — no rote memorising.

▶ Start learning Test myself first
1The big idea

Two sense organs, two familiar machines

Our environment changes constantly, and our sense organs — eyes, ears, nose, tongue, skin — detect those changes. This chapter is about the two most complex: the eye (our optical sensor) and the ear (our audio sensor).

👁️ The eye = a camera

A camera has a lens that focuses light onto film at the back. Your eye does exactly the same: a lens focuses light onto the retina at the back. Hold this analogy — every eye part maps onto a camera part.

👂 The ear = a microphone

A microphone catches sound waves on a thin membrane and turns the vibration into an electrical signal. Your ear catches sound on the eardrum and passes the vibration inward to become a nerve signal. Same job.

Your reading strategyDon't memorise part-lists cold. For each part, ask "what is its job in the machine?" The name will stick because the function makes sense. That's how this whole guide is built.

3How vision works

From light ray to "I see it"

To see an object, light from it must enter the eye, refract (bend) through the lens, and converge onto the retina, forming an image. Here's the twist that surprises most students:

The image on your retina is upside-down

The lens flips the image, so it lands on the retina inverted and smaller than the real object. Nerve endings on the retina fire, the signal travels along the optic nerve, and the visual area of the brain flips it back and interprets it as upright. So "seeing" is really the brain's interpretation of retina signals.

Say it in one lineLight → refracts through lens → inverted image on retina → optic nerve → brain interprets as upright.

Why the lens is convex — a 20-second lens lesson

Lenses bend light. This is the foundation for everything about vision defects, so let's nail it visually:

Animation · Convex vs Concave lenses

Convex lens: parallel light rays bend inward and meet at a single point — the focus. The distance from lens to focus is the focal length. The eye lens is convex.

🔵 Convex lens

Fatter in the middle. Converges rays to a real focus. Used in the eye and to correct long sight.

🟣 Concave lens

Thinner in the middle. Diverges rays (focus appears in front). Used to correct short sight.
4Accommodation

The clever trick: a lens that changes shape

Here's the puzzle. With a camera, to focus on near vs far objects you move the lens (change the image distance). But in your eye, the distance from lens to retina is fixed — it can't move. So how do you focus on both a book and a mountain?

Answer: the eye lens changes its curvature

The ciliary muscles squeeze or relax the lens to change how strongly it bends light. This automatic focusing is called accommodation. Watch it happen:

Simulator · Focusing near vs far

Far object: rays arrive nearly parallel. The lens flattens (curvature reduced, longer focal length) so they still land exactly on the retina.
ObjectRays arrive as…Eye lens does…
Distant 🏔️Parallel raysReduces curvature (flatter, longer focal length)
Close 📖Diverging raysIncreases curvature (fatter, shorter focal length)
Remember the ruleClose object → fatter lens. A near object needs more bending power, so the lens bulges. A far object needs less, so it flattens. This one rule explains both vision defects next.
5Defects of vision

When focusing goes wrong

Two common defects happen when the eyeball is too long/short, or the lens can't adjust its focal length properly. The whole game is where the image lands relative to the retina.

The simulator that makes it click

Simulator · Vision defects & their correction

Normal eye: the image forms exactly on the retina. Nice and sharp.

🔴 Short sight (Myopia)

Near objects clear, distant blurred.

Cause: eyeball too long, or lens curvature can't be reduced enough → image forms in front of the retina.

Correction: a concave (concave meniscus) lens diverges the rays first, so the eye lens then lands them on the retina.

🟠 Long sight (Hypermetropia)

Distant objects clear, near blurred.

Cause: eyeball too short, or lens can't increase curvature enough → image forms behind the retina.

Correction: a convex (convex meniscus) lens converges the rays first, helping focus on the retina.

Never mix these up: Short sight (myopia) → image in front → fix with Concave. Long sight → image behind → fix with Convex. Memory hook: "myopia = only near", concave for close-only eyes.
6Two eyes, one world

Binocular & stereoscopic vision

Why two eyes? Because overlapping views let your brain judge distance and depth. Notice where the eyes sit on different animals:

👤 Human / monkey — eyes in front
🐄 Cattle / dog / tiger — eyes on sides
Binocular visionThe ability to keep both eyes focused on one object, creating a single image. Humans, monkeys, chimpanzees, gorillas and loris have eyes in front → a broad binocular range.
Stereoscopic visionBecause of binocular vision we get stereoscopic vision — the ability to judge an object's distance, depth and height. (Try threading a pen into a cap with one eye vs two — two is far easier!)

Prey animals (cattle, dog, tiger) have side-facing eyes — less overlap, but each eye watches a wider separate area to spot danger.

7Eye diseases & care

Cataract, glaucoma & keeping eyes healthy

☁️ Cataract

The normally clear lens turns cloudy/milky because its proteins denature. Cloudy lens → light can't focus on the retina → everything looks blurred.

Causes: age, genetics, and UV rays reaching Earth through ozone-layer holes.

🕳️ Glaucoma

Gradually shrinks your field of vision and can lead to blindness by damaging the optic nerve. Usually caused by high pressure inside the eye; diabetics are at higher risk.

The damage is irreversible — but early detection can control it.

10Hearing limits & care

What your ears can take — and can't

Your audible range: 20 Hz – 20,000 Hz

Humans hear frequencies between 20 Hz and 20,000 Hz. Below or above that, you simply can't hear it. And within the range, sounds that are too intense (too loud) damage the ear.

20 Hz20,000 Hz

← infrasound (can't hear)  |  audible range  |  ultrasound (can't hear) →

Loudness danger meter (dB)

Sounds near and above the pain threshold (~130–140 dB, jet engine) can damage hearing quickly.

🔇 Defects of the ear

Impaired hearing, deafness, and hardening of the ossicles can occur from birth or during life. People deaf from birth are often mute too. Hearing aids can remedy loss of hearing.

🛡️ Protecting your ears

Don't push foreign objects into the ear · avoid loud noises · no ear medicine without advice · don't dive deep without protection (high pressure) · don't slap the ear or drag the ear lobe.

Balance bonus factThe semi-circular canals in the inner ear aren't for hearing at all — they keep your body balanced. Classic exam question!
11Last-minute revision

⚡ The night-before cheat sheet

Everything essential, condensed. Skim it the hour before the exam — tap back to any section you're unsure about.

👁️ Eye parts & jobs

  • Sclerotic: tough white outer layer.
  • Cornea: transparent front of sclerotic.
  • Choroid: supplies blood.
  • Retina: holds rod & cone (light-sensitive) cells.
  • Lens: biconvex, changes curvature, focuses on retina.
  • Iris: controls light entering. Pupil: hole in iris.
  • Ciliary muscle: holds lens, changes its curvature.
  • Aqueous (watery, front) & vitreous (jelly, keeps shape).
  • Fovea/yellow spot: sharpest vision. Blind spot: no vision.
  • Optic nerve: carries signal to brain.

🔬 How we see

  • Light → refracts through convex lens → converges on retina.
  • Image on retina is real, inverted, smaller.
  • Optic nerve → brain interprets as upright.
  • Accommodation: lens changes curvature to focus.
  • Far object → lens flatter. Close object → lens fatter.

👓 Vision defects

  • Short sight (myopia): near clear, far blurred. Image in front of retina. Eyeball too long. Fix: concave lens.
  • Long sight (hypermetropia): far clear, near blurred. Image behind retina. Eyeball too short. Fix: convex lens.

🦠 Eye diseases

  • Cataract: lens turns cloudy/milky (proteins denature). Causes: age, genetics, UV.
  • Glaucoma: optic nerve damage from high eye pressure → shrinking vision → blindness. Irreversible.
  • Sore eyes: viral; red, watery; spreads by contact/insects; curable.

👂 Ear parts (outer→inner)

  • Outer: Pinna (directs sound) → Ear canal → Tympanic membrane (eardrum, vibrates).
  • Middle: Ossicles (malleus, incus, stapes — transmit vibration); Eustachian tube (balances pressure, connects to pharynx).
  • Inner: Cochlea (nerve endings → auditory nerve); Auditory nerve (to brain); Semi-circular canals (balance).

🔊 Hearing & limits

  • Path: wave → canal → eardrum → ossicles → cochlea → auditory nerve → brain = hearing.
  • Audible range: 20 Hz – 20,000 Hz.
  • Very loud sounds damage the ear.
  • Defects: deafness, impaired hearing, ossicle hardening. Remedy: hearing aids.
Memory hooks:  • Ossicles = "MIS" (Malleus, Incus, Stapes).  • Myopia = image in front → concave; hypermetropia = behind → convex.  • Close object → curvier lens.  • Semi-circular canals = Steadiness (balance), not sound.  • Range: 20 to 20,000.
