Level 1 · the foundation

①A fraction is "how many equal parts you have"

Cut a whole into equal parts. The bottom number (denominator) says how many equal parts the whole is split into. The top number (numerator) says how many of them you're taking.

= 35

3 shaded parts out of 5 equal parts → three-fifths.

It works for groups too. A team of 3 boys and 2 girls is 5 people. The boys are 35 of the team — same idea, "3 out of 5 equal shares".
Level 2 · building on level 1

②Proper, improper & mixed numbers

Once you can count parts, you can go past a whole. That's where these three names come in — they're just different ways of writing the same amount.

Proper (less than 1)

Top smaller than bottom.

34   12   23

Improper (1 or more)

Top ≥ bottom.

32   85   55

One full circle + 38 of another  =  138  (mixed number)  =  118  (improper)

Swap between them freely. Mixed → improper: multiply the whole number by the denominator and add the top (112 = (1×2+1)/2 = 32). Improper → mixed: divide top by bottom; the quotient is the whole, the remainder is the new top.
Level 3 · the key to everything after

③Equivalent fractions — same amount, different clothes

Multiply or divide the top and bottom by the same non-zero number and the value doesn't change. This one move powers adding, subtracting and simplifying.

Simplest form just means you've divided out every common factor — the same amount written with the smallest numbers.
Level 4 · now we can combine them

④Adding & subtracting: make the bottoms match first

You can only add or subtract parts that are the same size. Same denominator? Just add the tops. Different denominators? Use Level 3 to give them a common bottom first.

Same bottom → easy

15 + 45 − 25 = 1+4−25 = 35

Different bottoms → convert

14 + 23 − 56 = 312 + 812 − 1012 = 112

Level 5 · the easiest operation

⑤Multiplying & the reciprocal

Multiplying is the friendliest: no common denominator needed. Tops × tops, bottoms × bottoms.

25 × 13 = 2×15×3 = 215

Reciprocal = flip it over. The reciprocal of ab is ba. Two numbers are reciprocals when they multiply to 1 — e.g. 2 × 12 = 1, so 12 and 2 are reciprocals. (Whole number 5 = 51, so its reciprocal is 15.)
Level 6 · division is secretly multiplication

⑥Dividing: flip the second one, then multiply

To divide by a fraction, multiply by its reciprocal. Dividing by 32 is the same as multiplying by 23.

43 ÷ 2 = 43 × 12 = 46 = 23

Mixed numbers first become improper: 127 ÷ 112 = 97 × 23 = 67

Why flipping works: "how many halves fit into 3?" is 3 ÷ ½ = 6 — dividing by a half doubles. Multiplying by the flipped fraction (×2) does exactly that.
Level 7 · the word that unlocks the chapter

⑦"of" just means "×"

12 of 100 is 50 — and 50 = 12 × 100. Whenever you see "of" between a fraction and an amount, replace it with a multiply sign.

12 of 13 = 12 × 13 = 16

Take a third of a bar, then take half of that — you land on one-sixth of the whole bar. See it move in the "of" grid below.

General rule: ab of cd = ab × cd. It works for whole numbers and mixed numbers too (turn mixed numbers improper first).
Level 8 · putting it all together

⑧BODMAS — the order of operations

When one expression mixes +, −, ×, ÷ and "of", the answer depends on the order you work in. BODMAS is that agreed order, top priority first.

BBrackets — do what's inside ( ) first
OOf — "of" acts like a bracket; do it next
DDivision
▲ ÷ and × are equal — do them left → right ▼
MMultiplication
AAddition
▲ + and − are equal — do them left → right ▼
SSubtraction
Two traps to remember: ÷ and × have the same rank (go left to right, don't always divide first); likewise + and − (go left to right). And "of" outranks ÷ and × — so in 4/3 of 3/2, do the "of" before any surrounding × or ÷.

⭐ One-glance cheat sheet

Add / Subtract

Same bottom → add/subtract tops. Different → make a common denominator first.

Multiply

Tops × tops, bottoms × bottoms. No common denominator needed.

Divide

Flip the second fraction (reciprocal), then multiply.

"of"

Replace "of" with ×. Mixed numbers → improper first.

Mixed ↔ improper

whole×bottom+top over bottom; or divide top by bottom for the reverse.

BODMAS

Brackets, Of, ÷ & × (left→right), + & − (left→right).
