Warm-up skill

①Substitution — swap the letter for a number

An algebraic expression like 2a + 3 is a recipe. Substituting means replacing each letter with a given value and working out the number that comes out.

Example: find 2a + 3 when a = ½.

2a + 3 = 2 × ½ + 3
= 1 + 3
= 4
Directed numbers need care. When the value is negative or a fraction, keep it in brackets while you substitute. For 5 − 6b with b = −⅔:  5 − 6 × (−⅔) = 5 + 4 = 9. A minus times a minus becomes a plus — that trips up more students than anything else in this chapter.
Two golden rules: ① a number written next to a letter means multiply (2a = 2 × a). ② substitute the value inside brackets, then apply the sign rules carefully. Play with this in the Substitution Machine.
Vocabulary

②Terms, expressions & binomials

A quick map of the words, so the rest of the chapter reads easily.

Algebraic term

A single chunk: a letter, or letters and numbers multiplied/divided. e.g. x, 2x, xy, −3ab.

Algebraic expression

Terms joined by + or −. e.g. x + y, 2a + xyz, 4 + x.

Binomial

Exactly two terms. e.g. x + 3, x − 5, 2x + y. ("bi" = two.)

This chapter's goal

Multiply two binomials, e.g. (x + 3)(x + 2), and write the answer as a tidy expression.

The main method

③Multiplying two binomials — every term meets every term

To expand (x + 3)(x + 2), each term in the first bracket multiplies each term in the second. Two terms × two terms = four products.

(x + 3)(x + 2)  =  x(x + 2) + 3(x + 2)
x·x = x² x·2 = 2x 3·x = 3x 3·2 = 6
= x² + 2x + 3x + 6  =  x² + 5x + 6

The two middle terms (2x and 3x) are "like terms", so we add them into 5x.

Like a handshake between two teams. Everyone in bracket one shakes hands with everyone in bracket two — no one is skipped. Four people paired up = four products. Collect the like terms at the end.
See why it works

④The area model — the picture behind the algebra

A rectangle of sides (x + a) and (x + b) splits into four smaller rectangles. Their areas are the four products — so the total area is the expansion.

Sides (x + 3) and (x + 2) give four tiles:

x²
3x
2x
6

total area = x² + 3x + 2x + 6 = x² + 5x + 6

The big square is x·x = x². The two side strips are 3x and 2x. The small corner is 3·2 = 6. Add them and you get the same answer as the algebra — that's the proof by area. Build any rectangle yourself in the Area Explorer.
The shortcut

⑤The pattern — and how signs behave

Once you've done a few, a pattern appears that lets you expand in one line.

(x + a)(x + b) = x² + (a + b)x + a·b

middle number = sum of the two numbers  ·  last number = their product

Both positive

(x+5)(x+3) = x² + 8x + 15

5+3 = 8,  5×3 = 15.

One negative

(x+5)(x−3) = x² + 2x − 15

5+(−3) = 2,  5×(−3) = −15.

The other negative

(x−5)(x+3) = x² − 2x − 15

(−5)+3 = −2,  (−5)×3 = −15.

Both negative

(x−5)(x−3) = x² − 8x + 15

(−5)+(−3) = −8,  (−5)×(−3) = +15.

Sign watch: two negatives multiply to a positive last term, but add to a negative middle term. And (x−5)(x+5) = x² − 25 — the middle vanishes because −5 + 5 = 0. Keep the signs glued to their numbers.
Check your work

⑥Verifying an expansion

Not sure your expansion is right? Pick a number for x, put it into both sides. If they match, your expansion is almost certainly correct.

Check (x + 8)(x − 3) = x² + 5x − 24 at x = 5:

Left side

(5 + 8)(5 − 3) = 13 × 2 = 26

Right side

5² + 5×5 − 24 = 25 + 25 − 24 = 26

Both give 26 → the expansion checks out. ✓

Why it works: if two expressions are truly equal, they must give the same value for every number you put in. Testing one value won't prove it beyond doubt, but it catches almost all mistakes — and it's fast.

⭐ One-glance cheat sheet

Substitution

Swap the letter for its value in brackets, then simplify. 2a means 2 × a.

Sign rules

− × − = +,  − × + = −. Subtracting a negative adds.

Expand (2 brackets)

Every term × every term = 4 products, then collect like terms.

The pattern

(x+a)(x+b) = x²+(a+b)x+ab
middle = sum, last = product.

Area model

Four tiles: x², ax, bx, ab. Their total area = the expansion.

Verify

Put one value of x into both sides; equal answers = correct.
