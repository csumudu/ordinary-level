The starting point

①Profit and loss: compare two prices

Every sale has two prices: the cost price (what the seller paid to make or buy the item) and the selling price (what they sold it for). Comparing them tells you everything.

Cost
Rs 1000
Sells for
Rs 2600

Selling price > cost → profit = 2600 − 1000 = Rs 1600

If selling price > cost → profit, and profit = selling price − cost.
If cost > selling price → loss, and loss = cost − selling price.
If they're equal → no profit, no loss.
Think of it as a tug-of-war between two prices. Whichever is bigger decides whether it's a profit or a loss, and the gap between them is how much. That's the whole idea — everything else in this chapter is built on this one comparison.
Scaling up

②Buying and selling a whole stock

Vendors rarely sell one item. Two easy ways to handle a whole stock — pick whichever feels natural.

Way 1 · Totals first

Work out total cost and total income, then compare.

Buy 50 coconuts @ Rs 45 → cost = 45×50 = Rs 2250
Sell 50 @ Rs 60 → income = 60×50 = Rs 3000
Profit = 3000 − 2250 = Rs 750

Way 2 · Per item first

Find profit on one item, then multiply by the quantity.

Profit per coconut = 60 − 45 = Rs 15
For 50 coconuts = 15 × 50 = Rs 750

Same answer — Way 2 is often quicker.

Watch out for mixed sales & leftovers. If a stock is sold in parts at different prices (or some is discarded), add up the actual money received across all parts, then compare to the total cost. Discarded goods still count in the cost — they were paid for.

To compare deals fairly, ask: how much profit per Rs 100 spent? That's exactly what a percentage is. Converting each profit into "per hundred" puts every deal on the same scale — that's why percentages are the language of buying and selling.
The formula

④Profit % and Loss %

Write the profit (or loss) as a fraction of the cost, then multiply by 100%. The cost is always the "base" — the thing we compare against.

profit % = profitcost price × 100%

loss % = losscost price × 100%

Profit example

Buy book Rs 25, sell Rs 30 → profit Rs 5.
profit % = (5 ÷ 25) × 100% = 20%

Loss example

Buy trousers Rs 500, sell Rs 450 → loss Rs 50.
loss % = (50 ÷ 500) × 100% = 10%
The #1 mistake: dividing by the selling price. Always divide by the cost (the buying price or production cost). Profit/loss is measured against what you put in.
Reverse gear

⑤Working backwards with the "Rs 100" trick

Often you're given the percentage and asked for a price. The cleanest way: imagine the cost is Rs 100 and scale up.

Find the selling price

Almirah costs Rs 30 000, sold at 15% profit.

If cost were Rs 100, it sells for Rs 115 (100 + 15).
So SP = 115100 × 30 000 = Rs 34 500

Loss works the same: 2% loss → sells for Rs 98 per Rs 100.

Find the cost (given SP + %)

TV sold at Rs 22 000, at 10% profit. Cost?

If cost were Rs 100, SP would be Rs 110.
So cost = 100110 × 22 000 = Rs 20 000

Loss of 15% → SP is Rs 85 per Rs 100, so cost = SP × 100/85.

The pattern in one line: profit p% means SP = cost × (100+p)/100; loss L% means SP = cost × (100−L)/100. To go from SP back to cost, flip the fraction.
Shopping

⑥Discounts — a cut off the marked price

The marked price is the price written on the item. A discount is an amount taken off it — usually given as a percentage of the marked price.

Example: books marked Rs 1500, discount 20%.

Discount = 1500 × 20100 = Rs 300
You pay = 1500 − 300 = Rs 1200   (or directly: 1500 × 80/100)

Discount % = (discount ÷ marked price) × 100%. To find the marked price from what you paid, use SP × 100/(100 − discount%).

Discount is to the marked price what loss is to the cost — a percentage subtracted. If the discount is 10%, you pay for 90 out of every 100 of the marked price. Same "Rs 100" trick as before.
Middlemen

⑦Commissions — a cut of the deal

When an agent (a broker) helps sell a house, car or land, they charge a commission — a percentage of the transaction value.

Find the commission

Car worth Rs 3 000 000, commission 5%.
= 3 000 000 × 5/100 = Rs 150 000

Find the commission %

Fee Rs 36 000 on land worth Rs 1 200 000.
= (36 000 ÷ 1 200 000) × 100% = 3%
Same shape as everything else in this chapter: commission = value × rate/100, and commission % = (commission ÷ value) × 100%. The seller's take-home is the sale value minus the commission.

⭐ One-glance cheat sheet

Profit / Loss

profit = SP − cost. loss = cost − SP. Bigger price wins; the gap is the amount.

Profit % / Loss %

(profit or loss) ÷ cost × 100%. Always divide by the cost, never the SP.

SP from cost + %

profit p%: SP = cost × (100+p)/100. loss L%: cost × (100−L)/100.

Cost from SP + %

profit p%: cost = SP × 100/(100+p). loss L%: SP × 100/(100−L).

Discount

discount = marked × d/100. You pay marked × (100−d)/100.

Commission

commission = value × c/100. Seller keeps value − commission.
